# Methodology

## 1. Research design

This project answers one core research question with a supporting operational
system:

> **RQ (core):** For intermittent engineered-component demand with stochastic
> supplier lead times, does nonparametric (KDE-based) safety-stock dimensioning
> achieve target service levels at lower total cost than the textbook
> normal-distribution approximation?

Supporting questions: (RQ2) what is the cost-vs-service Pareto frontier?
(RQ3) how much safety stock is driven by lead-time vs demand variability?
(RQ4) is the ranking robust to cost assumptions? (RQ5) how do policies behave
under disruption?

The operational half (shortage engine + risk module) is evaluated by
correctness against hand-checkable cases and by policy comparison under
identical simulated scenarios — not by claimed real-world savings.

## 2. Data

All data is synthetic, generated by `src/data_generation/generate_data.py`
(seed 42, parameters in `config/project_config.yaml`): 40 products, 200
materials, 20 suppliers in 3 behaviour tiers, 24 months of history
(2024-10-01 → 2026-09-30), ~1,500 production orders, ~2,700 PO lines,
~3,200 receipts, ~14,500 inventory transactions, plus a labeled 6-week
supplier disruption and quality-rejection processes. Business rules
(partial receipts, rejections excluded from usable stock, single-sourcing)
are causal, not independent noise. Ten automated integrity checks pass
(FK integrity, date logic, quantity rules, inventory sanity, BOM coverage).

## 3. Temporal split (anti-leakage)

- **Train:** 2024-10-01 → 2026-03-31 (18 months) — all estimation.
- **Test:** 2026-04-01 → 2026-09-30 (6 months) — never used in estimation.
- Monte Carlo bootstraps demand from train and resamples empirical lead times;
  a separate direct backtest runs on the test period.

## 4. Safety-stock methods

**Baselines:** (B1) 4 weeks-of-cover rule of thumb; (B2) normal approximation
SS = z·σ_d·√(LT̄+R); (B3) min-max (min = B2 reorder point, max = +30d demand).

**Proposed:** bootstrap lead-time-demand (LTD) samples — each sample draws a
lead time from the empirical LT distribution, then sums a contiguous demand
block of (LT+R) days, capturing *both* demand and lead-time stochasticity
(the textbook formula assumes fixed LT). Then:
- (P1) empirical quantile: SS = Q_SL(LTD) − mean(LTD)
- (P2) KDE: Gaussian KDE (Silverman bandwidth) on LTD samples; SS from the
  KDE quantile, floored at 0; negative draws clipped (demand ≥ 0).

**(R,S) policy:** weekly review; order up to S = mean_LTD + SS.

## 5. Simulation

Discrete-time (R,S) simulator (`src/simulation/simulator.py`): stochastic
demand (bootstrapped), stochastic lead times (resampled actuals), defective
receipt fractions, backordering. Warmup 30 days discarded. Metrics —
fill rate β = satisfied/demanded; cycle service level α = fraction of review
cycles with no stockout; stockout days; average inventory value; total modeled
cost = holding (22%/yr) + ordering (₹1,500) + stockout penalty (2.5× unit
cost). Scenario engine scales demand/LT/defect per `scenario_config`.

## 6. Risk methodology

Follows the brief's ISO-31000-style lifecycle. Risk features are computed from
data (OTD %, lateness, rejection rate, single-source flag, days-of-cover).
Probability-impact 1–5 scales are **project-defined ordinal cutoffs**
(1–4 Low, 5–9 Moderate, 10–16 High, 17–25 Critical) — documented as such, never
treated as calibrated probabilities. Expected loss = empirical frequency ×
impact **only** where n≥20 events support a frequency; otherwise the ordinal
score stands alone. Mitigation actions are constrained to qualified suppliers.

## 7. Statistical reporting

Paired comparisons across the 30-material stratified sample (each material is
one pair): mean difference, t-based 95% CI, Wilcoxon signed-rank p. Monte
Carlo gives per-cell 95% CIs (2.5/97.5 percentiles over replications).
Negative and null results are reported (E3, wide CIs in E1).
